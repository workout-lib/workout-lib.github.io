### Step 1
Body movement:
Your body moves in a wave-like motion: when your shoulders press down, your hips and legs rise up, and vice versa. This wave motion alone already helps push you forward through the water.

### Step 2
Keep your legs together and kick up and down in a wave that starts from your shoulders and travels through your whole body. The first kick is light—at this moment your upper body and head move forward and up. Then comes a stronger kick—at this moment your back rises and your head and shoulders drop into the water. The kick comes from your hips; avoid bending your knees too much.

### Step 3
Arms (pull and recovery):
Start with your arms at your sides (glide phase), then sweep them out to the sides and pull through the water. Unlike breaststroke, where the pull stops at shoulder level, in butterfly you pull all the way down to your hips. After that, lift your arms out of the water and swing them forward over the surface.

### Step 4
Breathing:
The power of your pull and the wave motion lift your shoulders and head above the water—take a quick, strong breath through your mouth. Then, as your arms swing forward, lower your face back into the water. Exhale steadily through your nose (and mouth if needed) throughout the rest of the stroke cycle.

{{% /steps %}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
⠀• Kick from your hips and try not to bend your knees too much.
⠀• Don't lift your arms too high during recovery—it only slows you down.
⠀• When your arms swing forward, keep them shoulder-width apart. Don't let them cross or clap together, as this creates drag.
⠀• When breathing, lift your chin no more than 2–3 inches (7–8 cm) above the water—otherwise you push yourself up instead of forward.
⠀• To improve your body wave, make sure your chest is actively pressing down during the recovery.
⠀• Learn other strokes first. Butterfly should be the last stroke you attempt—it demands a lot of strength and endurance that you need to build beforehand.
⠀• You can wear fins to make learning easier, but do not use a kickboard—it prevents you from feeling the full body wave.
⠀• Butterfly is very tiring, so avoid eating a large meal before your workout to prevent stomach cramps.
