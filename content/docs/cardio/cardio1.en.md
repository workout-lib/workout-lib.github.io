### Step 1
Keep your chest up and your shoulders back; do not slouch. Keep your abdominal and back muscles engaged, as if you were preparing to take a deep breath.

### Step 2
Running is essentially a continuous forward fall: you fall forward and place your feet under you to keep yourself from actually falling. This allows you to conserve energy by using the momentum of the fall to help propel you forward.

### Step 3
Place your feet correctly under your falling body: land on the forefoot, ideally directly beneath your body's center of gravity.

### Step 4
Beginners usually have a fairly low running cadence (number of steps per minute), often taking overly long strides and bouncing too high. Aim to move your feet more frequently (around 90 steps per minute) and take shorter strides.

### Step 5
Keep your arms bent at a 90-degree angle and move them alongside your body.

### Step 6
Step-by-step sequence: push off the ground with the first foot, shift your body's center of gravity to the second leg, land on the first foot while lifting the second foot off the ground, shift your center of gravity to the first leg, land on the second foot, and repeat the sequence.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
⠀Wear comfortable clothing and athletic shoes.
⠀Run in areas with clean air. Avoid running alongside busy roads.
⠀Always start at a slow pace, gradually increase your speed, and slow down again toward the end of your run.
